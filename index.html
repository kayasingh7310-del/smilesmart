Understand your oral health

Explore common questions using simple, teen-friendly explanations.

🦠
Conditions

Why cavities happen

Bacteria use sugars from foods and drinks to produce acids that can weaken enamel.

Frequent exposure matters because each sugary snack or drink can start another acid attack. Fluoride toothpaste, water, flossing, and routine dental care can help lower risk.

🪥
Daily Care

Better brushing

Use gentle circular motions and reach the outer, inner, and chewing surfaces.

A soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste are common recommendations. Replace a worn toothbrush and avoid brushing so hard that your gums become irritated.

🧵
Daily Care

How flossing helps

Floss cleans spaces that toothbrush bristles may not reach effectively.

Guide the floss gently around each tooth in a C shape. Avoid snapping it into your gums. Floss picks or water flossers may be useful alternatives for some people.

🥤
Nutrition

Sugar and acid

Many sweet drinks are also acidic, which can increase stress on enamel.

Sipping a sugary drink for hours repeatedly exposes teeth. Water is a tooth-friendly choice, and consuming sweet drinks less often may reduce exposure.

🩸
Conditions

Bleeding gums

Bleeding can happen when gums are irritated or inflamed.

Gentle brushing and consistent cleaning between teeth may help. Persistent bleeding, swelling, pain, or worsening symptoms should be evaluated by a dental professional.

😁
Daily Care

Caring for braces

Brackets and wires create extra spaces where food and plaque can collect.

Clean carefully around brackets, use tools recommended by your orthodontist, and follow instructions about foods and appointments.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers

How often should I replace my toothbrush?

Many dental organizations recommend replacing it every three to four months, or sooner if the bristles are frayed. Follow your dentist’s guidance for your individual needs.

Is mouthwash required?

Mouthwash can be useful for some people, but it does not replace brushing and cleaning between teeth. A dentist can help determine whether a specific product is appropriate.

Why do my teeth feel sensitive?

Sensitivity can have several causes, including exposed dentin, enamel wear, decay, or gum changes. Persistent or severe sensitivity should be checked by a dental professional.

Can a cavity heal on its own?

Very early mineral loss may sometimes be strengthened with fluoride and improved habits, but an established cavity generally requires professional evaluation and treatment.

What should I do about severe tooth pain or swelling?

Contact a dentist promptly. Facial swelling, breathing or swallowing difficulty, uncontrolled bleeding, or serious injury may require urgent medical care.
